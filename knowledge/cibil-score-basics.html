CIBIL Score: What Affects It?

Your credit report records credit accounts and repayment behaviour reported by lenders. A CIBIL Score is a three-digit summary derived from information in the report. Lenders may consider the score along with income, existing obligations, documents and their own credit policy; a score alone does not guarantee approval.

Key factors that influence a score

  • Payment history: Late or missed payments can negatively affect the credit profile. Pay EMIs and card dues by their due dates.
  • Credit utilisation: Using a large share of available revolving credit may signal higher dependence on credit. Keep balances manageable and pay in full where possible.
  • Age of credit: The length of time accounts have been active contributes to the picture of your credit history.
  • Credit enquiries: Multiple applications in a short period can create several lender enquiries. Apply when there is a genuine need and compare options thoughtfully.

Practical steps

  1. Review your credit report periodically and check account details, outstanding balances and repayment status.
  2. Raise a dispute through the credit bureau and/or lender if information appears inaccurate.
  3. Set payment reminders or autopay with sufficient account balance.
  4. Avoid taking fresh credit only to try to increase a score.

Does a high score guarantee a loan?

No. Each lender applies its own eligibility and underwriting criteria. Income stability, existing EMIs, property or collateral, employment/business profile, documentation and loan policy may also matter.
